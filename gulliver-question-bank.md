## LO 1-1 — Profit, Risk, and Stakeholders

**1. [micro-check · easy (-1)]** In general, how are risk and potential profit related in business?
- A) They have nothing to do with each other
- B) Bigger risk generally needs the potential for bigger profit to be worth taking  ✓
- C) Higher risk always guarantees higher profit
- D) Profit only exists when there is zero risk
_Answer: B — Risk and potential profit move together: the more an entrepreneur risks, the bigger the potential payoff usually needs to be for that risk to make sense._

**2. [micro-check · easy (-1.5)]** Which of these is a stakeholder in a business?
- A) Only the people who own the company itself
- B) Only the paying customers who buy from it

_Answer: C — Stakeholders are everyone affected by or invested in the business, a much wider group than just owners or customers._

**3. [applied · medium (0.5)]** Why does the bank care about how much risk Maria is taking on with the second truck?
- A) Because, as a stakeholder, it needs her business profitable enough to repay the loan  ✓

- C) Because a bank stops being a stakeholder the moment the loan paperwork is signed
- D) Because the bank is mainly worried about competing with Maria's food truck for customers
_Answer: A — The bank is a stakeholder with a direct financial interest: it needs Maria's business to generate enough profit to repay what she borrowed._

## LO 1-4 — Technology's Effects on Business

**1. [micro-check · medium (-0.5)]** What does 'efficiency' mean in a business context?
- A) Doing the right thing in the right way
- B) Producing items using the least amount of resources  ✓
- C) The amount of output per hour worked
- D) The total money a business brings in from sales
_Answer: B — Efficiency is about minimizing waste: producing items using the least amount of resources._

**2. [micro-check · medium (0)]** A factory installs new equipment and now produces twice as many units per hour with the same number of workers. Which concept BEST describes this gain?
- A) Effectiveness, because it means finally making the products customers actually want
- B) Efficiency, because it means the same products are made with less waste
- C) Productivity, because more output is coming from the same amount of work  ✓
- D) Profitability, because doubling the units produced must mean much higher profit
_Answer: C — Productivity is output for a given amount of input, so more units per hour from the same workers is a productivity gain. Efficiency is the tempting one, but it specifically means using fewer resources for the same output, not getting more output per worker._

**3. [applied · hard (1)]** Why did the new ordering system improve efficiency specifically, not just productivity?
- A) It cut the wasted food from wrong orders, so fewer resources are used per meal  ✓
- B) It let the kitchen serve more tables per hour, which is really productivity
- C) It mainly made the servers work faster than they used to before
- D) It raised the price of each meal, so the restaurant keeps more per order
_Answer: A — Cutting down on wasted food from mistaken orders is specifically an efficiency gain: using fewer resources per unit produced._

**4. [mastery-check · medium (-0.5)]** What is 'effectiveness'?

- B) Producing each item using the least possible amount of resources and waste

- D) How quickly a task gets done, whether or not it was the right task
_Answer: A — Effectiveness is about doing the RIGHT thing, in the right way, not just doing things quickly or cheaply._

- B) How much a business chooses to spend on advertising
