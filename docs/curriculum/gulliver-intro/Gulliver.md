# Gulliver Introduction to Business — Classroom Integration Plan

**Course:** Byrnes intro business, Ch 1–2
**Format:** 85-minute blocks, 3 per chapter, 6 total
**Students:** laptops in class
**Teacher posture:** flexible on structure — we have latitude here

---

## The 85-minute template

Same rhythm every block, so nobody has to learn a new routine.

| Time | Segment | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| — | **Pre-class primer** (5 min, homework) | student, at home |
| 0–5 | Warm-up: Daily Scenario, leaderboard projected | InvestiPlay |
| 5–30 | Lecture segment 1 | Byrnes |
| 30–35 | Checkpoint A — 3 questions, live class distribution | InvestiPlay |
| 35–55 | Lecture segment 2 | Byrnes |
| 55–60 | Checkpoint B — 3 questions | InvestiPlay |
| 60–80 | Lecture segment 3 or applied activity | either |
| 80–85 | Exit ticket — 2 questions, feeds mastery scores | InvestiPlay |
| — | **Post-class practice** (10 min, homework) | student, at home |

Total in-class InvestiPlay time: **15 of 85 minutes.** That number matters
when you pitch it — you're asking for 18% of the block, and giving back
better-prepped students plus live data he doesn't currently have.

The checkpoints are the load-bearing piece. Byrnes pauses, students answer
on laptops, and he sees the distribution on his screen before he decides
whether to move on. That's the thing he cannot do today.

## Block-by-block content map

### Chapter 1

**Block 1 — What a business is, and why risk pays**
Covers: business, goods vs. services, entrepreneur, revenue/profit/loss,
risk and reward, standard of living vs. quality of life, stakeholders,
outsourcing/insourcing, nonprofits.

- Primer: goods vs. services sorting drill, revenue/profit/loss definitions
- Checkpoint A: profit vs. revenue (the classic confusion)
- Checkpoint B: standard of living vs. quality of life
- Activity slot: risk/reward scenario — pick a venture, weigh both
- Practice: the nine terms from this block, adaptive

*Heaviest vocabulary block in either chapter. Nine terms in 85 minutes.
This is where the primer earns its place.*

**Block 2 — Entrepreneurs, factors of production, economic and tech environment**
Covers: trade-offs of entrepreneurship, the five factors of production,
economic/legal environment, technological environment, e-commerce,
databases, identity theft.

- Primer: the five factors, with an example of each
- Checkpoint A: classify resources into the five factors
- Checkpoint B: which government actions help or hinder business
- Activity slot: identify B2C vs. B2B in companies students actually use
- Practice: factors of production + economic environment

**Block 3 — Competitive, social, global, ecological environments; evolution of US business**
Covers: competitive environment, demography and diversity, aging
population, Gen Z as consumers, globalization, geopolitical risk, climate
and greening, agriculture → manufacturing → service → information.

- Primer: the four-era progression of the US economy
- Checkpoint A: match a business decision to which environment drove it
- Checkpoint B: era identification
- Activity slot: Gen Z consumer discussion — they *are* the case study
- Practice: mixed review across all of Chapter 1

### Chapter 2

**Block 4 — What economics is; capitalism's foundations**
Covers: economics, macro vs. micro, resource development, Malthus, Adam
Smith, invisible hand, capitalism, state capitalism, four basic rights.

- Primer: macro vs. micro sorting
- Checkpoint A: classify questions as macro or micro
- Checkpoint B: the four rights
- Activity slot: invisible hand — self-interest producing public benefit
- Practice: foundations vocabulary

**Block 5 — Supply, demand, equilibrium; market structures; other systems**
Covers: supply, demand, equilibrium/market price, perfect and monopolistic
competition, oligopoly, monopoly, benefits and limits of free markets,
socialism, communism, mixed economies.

- Primer: supply and demand as opposing curves
- Checkpoint A: shift a curve, predict the new price
- Checkpoint B: name the market structure from a description
- Activity slot: **interactive supply/demand tool** — drag the curves, watch
  equilibrium move. Build this one properly; it's the single best visual
  concept in either chapter.
- Practice: market structures + economic systems

*Densest conceptual block. Three figures in the deck are devoted to
equilibrium alone — that's the signal for where to invest build effort.*

**Block 6 — The US economy: indicators, cycles, policy**
Covers: GDP, gross output, unemployment rate and its four types, inflation/
disinflation/deflation/stagflation, CPI, core inflation, PPI, productivity,
business cycle, fiscal policy, Keynes, deficit and national debt, monetary
policy and the Fed.

- Primer: GDP, unemployment, CPI — the three headline numbers
- Checkpoint A: identify the business-cycle phase from a description
- Checkpoint B: fiscal vs. monetary — who acts, what tool
- Activity slot: **stock simulator tie-in** — the Fed raises rates, what
  happens to your portfolio? This is where InvestiPlay does something the
  textbook structurally can't.
- Practice: full Chapter 2 review, weighted toward this block

*~18 defined terms. Also the block that connects most naturally to features
you already have.*

## Teacher-side requirements

Ranked by how much they determine whether the pilot renews.

1. **Class mastery view** — per-concept distribution for the section, with
   the weakest 3–4 concepts surfaced before an assessment. Thirty seconds to
   read, immediately actionable. This is the product.
2. **Live checkpoint results** — mid-lecture, on his screen, while he can
   still act on it.
3. **Assign by block** — pick a block, push primer and practice, set a due
   date.
4. **Completion at a glance** — who did the homework.
5. Everything else.

Build #1 and #2 first. #3 and #4 are table stakes but don't win anything.
